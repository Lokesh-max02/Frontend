TN_Election

The election in Tamil Nadu is an important democratic process where people choose their representatives and government. Elections are mainly conducted for the Legislative Assembly, Parliament, and local bodies. The Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly election is usually held once every five years.
During the election, political parties campaign across the state to gain public support and present their promises for development, welfare, education, employment, infrastructure, and social welfare schemes. Some of the major political parties in Tamil Nadu are Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK), All India Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (AIADMK), Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), and Indian National Congress (Congress). These parties contest elections by announcing manifestos that explain their future plans and policies for the people. Political leaders conduct rallies, public meetings, and social media campaigns to attract voters. The election process in Tamil Nadu is managed by the Election Commission of India.
Citizens who are 18 years and above have the right to vote. Voting is usually done through Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs), which help conduct elections quickly and accurately. The election process includes voter registration, campaigning, polling, counting of votes, and declaration of results.
Tamil Nadu elections are known for high voter participation and strong political awareness among the people. Elections play a major role in strengthening democracy because they allow citizens to select leaders who can work for the development of the state.
Through elections, people can express their opinions and choose a government that represents their needs and expectations.

Election_history

The history of elections in Tamil Nadu reflects the growth of democracy and political change in the state. Before 1969, Tamil Nadu was known as Madras State. In the early years after Indian independence, the Indian National Congress dominated politics in the state. Leaders such as K. Kamaraj played an important role in the development of education, industries, and infrastructure during this period.
A major political change happened in 1967 when the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK), led by C. N. Annadurai, won the Assembly election and formed the government. This victory ended Congress rule in the state and marked the rise of Dravidian politics. The DMK promoted Tamil language, social justice, and regional identity, which gained strong public support.
Later, in 1972, M. G. Ramachandran founded the All India Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (AIADMK) after separating from the DMK. Under his leadership, the AIADMK became one of the strongest political parties in Tamil Nadu. After MGR’s death, J. Jayalalithaa became the leader of AIADMK and served as Chief Minister multiple times. On the DMK side, M. Karunanidhi remained an influential leader for several decades.
Tamil Nadu politics has mainly been dominated by the DMK and AIADMK for many years. Elections in the state are often highly competitive, with alliances involving national parties like the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and Congress. The state is known for high voter turnout and active public participation in elections.
In recent years, new leaders and political changes have continued to shape Tamil Nadu elections. The election process is conducted by the Election Commission of India using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs). The history of Tamil Nadu elections shows the importance of democracy, regional politics, and public involvement in shaping the government and development of the state.

Result

The most recent major election in Tamil Nadu was the 2021 Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly election. In this election, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK) alliance achieved a major victory and formed the government after defeating the All India Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (AIADMK) alliance. The results were announced on May 2, 2021.
The DMK won 133 seats on its own in the 234-member Assembly, while its alliance secured a total of 159 seats, which was well above the majority mark of 118 seats. The AIADMK won 66 seats and became the main opposition party.
Other parties such as the Indian National Congress, Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), PMK, CPI, and CPI(M) also won a few seats.
After the victory, M. K. Stalin became the Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu for the first time. The election marked the return of the DMK to power after ten years of AIADMK rule from 2011 to 2021. The election also showed high voter participation, with around 73% voter turnout across the state.
The results reflected important issues such as development, employment, welfare schemes, education, healthcare, and public opinion about the previous government. Tamil Nadu elections are considered highly competitive because the people actively participate in the democratic process and carefully choose their leaders.
The election process was conducted by the Election Commission of India using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs). The results demonstrated the strength of democracy in Tamil Nadu and the importance of public voting in shaping the government of the state. Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK) — around 108 seats Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK alliance) — around 73 seats All India Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (AIADMK alliance) — around 53 seats Important points: TVK became the single largest party but was slightly below the majority mark of 118 seats. M. K. Stalin and DMK faced major losses. This election was considered historic because it broke the long-time DMK vs AIADMK dominance in Tamil Nadu politics.
